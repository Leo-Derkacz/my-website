Paracelsus

Paracelsus was a Swiss physician, alchemist, lay theologian, and philosopher of the German Renaissance. He was a pioneer in several aspects of the medical revolution of the Renaissance, emphasizing the value of observation in combination with received wisdom. He is often credited as the "father of toxicology".

The Bubonic Plague Quick Facts

This video gives some helpful background information:

  • The Bubonic Plague, also called the Black Death, was a sweeping pandemic across Europe that killed an estimated 25-50 million people, almost half of Europe's population at the time.
  • It is widely considered the deadliest pandemic in history (when adjusted as percentage of global population).
  • The bacterium that was responsible for the sickness was called Yersinia Pestis, a pathogen also responsible for the Plague of Justinian, another deadly pandemic some 800 years before the Black Death.
  • Although it mainly originated and spread through rats and other vermin, the explanations people at the time came up with ranged anywhere from astrology to antisemitism.
